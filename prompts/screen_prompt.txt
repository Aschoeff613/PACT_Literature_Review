You are screening records for a review whose goal is a taxonomy of HIGH-RISK
COGNITIVE TASKS in adult emergency medicine and primary care.

A taxonomy entry is a COGNITIVE DEMAND applied to a SPECIFIC CLINICAL SITUATION.
Examples of real entries:
  - "time-critical pattern recognition" applied to "sepsis in an ambiguous presentation"
  - "belief updating" applied to "revising a working diagnosis when new data conflicts"
  - "therapeutic inertia resistance" applied to "titrating medication in chronic disease"

So a paper is useful if it supplies EITHER half. Decide which:

TRACK A - supplies a cognitive demand.
  Names or defines a cognitive process in clinician reasoning (belief updating,
  signal extraction, risk stratification, metacognitive monitoring, task resumption).
  Setting does not matter much for this track. A nephrology or ICU paper that
  defines a transferable cognitive process still counts. INCLUDE it.

TRACK B - supplies a high-risk situation.
  Documents a specific clinical situation, decision, condition, or workflow point
  in adult ED or primary care where clinician reasoning fails, is difficult, or
  where error or delay causes harm. Look for: missed or delayed diagnosis of a
  named condition, undertriage, disposition error, handoff failure, failure to
  follow up a result, treatment inertia, conflicting guidelines in multimorbidity.
  What makes this track valuable is EVIDENCE OF RISK: an error rate, a harm or
  mortality figure, a claims analysis, a frequency.

TRACK C - both.

EXCLUDE only if the paper supplies neither: no cognitive process named, and no
specific high-risk situation with any evidence of difficulty or harm.

Do NOT exclude a paper merely because:
  - it is in a different specialty (Track A does not care about setting)
  - it studies AI or LLM reasoning (that is this program's subject matter; if it
    compares model and clinician reasoning or names a failure mode, INCLUDE it)
  - it is a case report (a case report of a missed diagnosis is Track B evidence)
  - the participants are trainees (note it, but include)

WHEN UNCERTAIN, INCLUDE.

Two things to get right in your answer. Do not return generic theory labels like
"diagnostic reasoning" or "clinical reasoning" alone: those are too broad to be a
taxonomy entry and we already have them. Give the most SPECIFIC formulation the
paper supports. And name the clinical situation concretely when there is one.
