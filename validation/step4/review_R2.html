How to review these abstracts

You are reviewer R2. This page holds your 100 papers. Read the title and abstract, then choose Include or Exclude.

Is clinician reasoning, decision-making, communication, or judgment-dependent work something the paper actually examines?

1. Cognitive task

The thinking itself: noticing, interpreting, recognizing, weighing uncertainty, prioritizing, deciding, communicating, or reconciling information.

2. Clinical task requiring judgment

The care activity where that thinking occurs: triage, diagnosis, choosing tests or treatment, admission or discharge, handoff, referral, medication review, or follow-up.

Simple rule: Ask what the paper is studying. A medical decision happening in the background is not enough.

Clear examples

EXCLUDEIs antibiotic A more effective than antibiotic B?
INCLUDEWhy do clinicians select antibiotic A rather than antibiotic B?
EXCLUDEHow often does antibiotic A cause adverse effects?
INCLUDEDoes a decision-support alert change antibiotic selection?

When genuinely uncertain, include the paper for later review.

Exclude: pediatric, dental, ICU/inpatient-only, prehospital, nursing-only or specialty-only work; case reports; editorials; papers about how patients think or decide rather than clinicians; simulations, vignettes, standardized patients, or chart-stimulated recall; and task-difficulty/theory papers with no clinician or clinician-behavior data. The review requires real patient care.

What counts?

Include when

The paper actually examines clinician reasoning, decisions, communication, or judgment-dependent work.

1. Cognitive task

The thinking itself: interpreting, recognizing, weighing uncertainty, prioritizing, deciding, communicating, or reconciling.

2. Clinical task requiring judgment

Where the thinking happens: triage, diagnosis, choosing tests or treatment, admission or discharge, handoff, referral, medication review, or follow-up.

Setting

Adult emergency department or adult primary care. A clinical activity counts only when the abstract also shows clinician thinking or judgment.

Exclude

The paper only asks whether a treatment or test works, reports outcomes or adverse effects, or never examines clinician work. Also exclude pediatric-only and dental papers; papers about how patients think or decide rather than clinicians; simulations, vignettes, standardized patients, chart-stimulated recall; and task-difficulty/theory papers with no clinician or clinician-behavior data; real patient care is required.
